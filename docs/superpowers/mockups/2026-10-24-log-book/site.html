Philosophy

Add weight to the bar. That's the principle.

Getting stronger means lifting more weight over time. Not muscle confusion. Not the burn. Not a new program every six weeks. Did the weight on the bar go up? Yes or no.

Objections
Only three exercises? That's not enough.

For a beginner it's plenty. Those three work your whole body. More exercises just split your focus and your recovery.

What about arms, abs and calves?

Squats and deadlifts hit your core harder than any ab exercise. Pressing grows your arms. Want accessories? Do them after the main work.

Three days isn't enough volume.

If you're adding weight every session, it's enough. Train hard three days, recover four.

This seems too simple to work.

Run it for three months and look at your log. Then tell me.

After that

Progressive overload: the only principle that matters.

Take one thing from this: progressive overload is what makes you stronger. Not muscle confusion. Not the burn. Not training to failure. Not "optimal" rep ranges.

What it is

Lift more weight over time. That's it. Squat 135 for 3 sets of 5 today, squat 225 for 3 sets of 5 in six months, and you got stronger. Your body adapted because the load went up.

How to do it

If you're new, add weight every session:

Squat · 3 × 5Linear progression
Session 1135 lb✓
Session 2140 lb✓
Session 3145 lb✓

Keep going until you miss reps. Then try it again.

When that stops working

Eventually you can't add weight every session. That's normal. You're an intermediate now. Add weight weekly, add reps before weight, and plan your heavy and light weeks. The rule doesn't change: the load has to go up.

Mistakes

Chasing the pump. It feels good. It doesn't make you stronger. Changing exercises all the time. You can't add weight to a lift you only did once. Obsessing over accessories. Squat, bench, deadlift. Add weight. The rest is extra.

Simple. Not easy. Go add five pounds.
